Direct Market Access for Farmers

August 5, 2024•4 min read

How digital platforms are connecting farmers directly with consumers, eliminating middlemen and ensuring fair prices for agricultural products.

For decades, Nepali farmers have been at the mercy of a complex chain of middlemen — local traders, district wholesalers, and urban distributors — each taking a cut before produce reaches the consumer. A farmer who grows tomatoes worth NPR 100 at retail often receives only NPR 25–35. Digital platforms are changing this fundamentally.

The Middleman Problem

The traditional agricultural supply chain in Nepal involves 3–5 intermediaries between farm and consumer. Each layer adds cost, time, and risk of spoilage.

  • Farmers typically receive only 25–40% of the final consumer price
  • Each intermediary adds 15–25% markup to cover their costs and profit
  • Post-harvest losses increase with each additional handling step
  • Farmers have no visibility into what buyers are actually paying

How Direct Access Changes Everything

When farmers list their produce directly on Krishi Connect, they set their own prices based on real market data. Verified buyers — restaurants, supermarkets, exporters, and individual consumers — can browse, compare, and purchase directly. The farmer receives payment digitally within 24 hours of delivery confirmation.

This direct connection doesn't just improve income — it also gives farmers valuable feedback about what buyers want, enabling them to make better decisions about what to grow next season.

Success Stories from the Platform

Farmers in Dhading who joined Krishi Connect's direct marketplace in 2024 reported average income increases of 45–60% compared to selling through traditional channels. One vegetable farmer from Benighat increased his monthly income from NPR 18,000 to NPR 31,000 simply by selling directly to three restaurants in Kathmandu.

What You Need to Get Started

Getting started on Krishi Connect's marketplace requires only a smartphone and a registered account. The platform handles payment processing, buyer verification, and dispute resolution. Logistics support is available for farmers who need help with delivery to urban buyers.
