Adapting to Climate Change in Agriculture

August 8, 2024•5 min read

Strategies and techniques that help farmers adapt to changing weather patterns and maintain productive farming operations.

Nepal is one of the most climate-vulnerable countries in the world. Glaciers are retreating, monsoon patterns are shifting, and extreme weather events — droughts, floods, and unseasonal frosts — are becoming more frequent. For farmers, this is not a distant threat. It is already affecting harvests today.

How Climate Change is Affecting Nepal's Farms

The impacts are varied and region-specific. In the Terai, erratic monsoons are causing both flash floods and mid-season droughts in the same year. In the hills, apple orchards that once thrived are struggling as winter temperatures rise.

  • Monsoon onset is now 2–3 weeks later than historical averages in many districts
  • Frost events are occurring at lower altitudes, damaging subtropical crops
  • Pest and disease ranges are expanding as temperatures rise
  • Soil erosion is accelerating due to more intense rainfall events

Adaptation Strategies That Work

Crop diversification — growing multiple varieties with different climate tolerances — reduces the risk of total crop failure. Shifting planting dates based on updated weather forecasts can avoid the worst of erratic weather windows.

Climate-resilient crop varieties developed by Nepal's agricultural research institutions are now available through Krishi Connect's seed network. These varieties are bred to tolerate drought, flooding, and temperature extremes better than traditional varieties.

Using Weather Data to Farm Smarter

Krishi Connect's weather integration provides hyperlocal forecasts that help farmers make better decisions about planting, irrigation, and harvest timing. Instead of relying on traditional seasonal calendars that no longer reflect current climate reality, farmers can access 10-day forecasts and seasonal outlooks tailored to their specific district.

Building Long-Term Resilience

Soil health is the foundation of climate resilience. Healthy soils with high organic matter content absorb water more effectively during floods and retain moisture longer during droughts. Investing in composting, cover cropping, and reduced tillage builds this resilience over time.
