Soil Testing and Nutrient Management

August 3, 2024•4 min read

Understanding soil health is crucial for sustainable farming. Learn about soil testing methods and how to manage nutrients effectively.

Most Nepali farmers apply fertilizers based on habit or guesswork — the same amount their parents used, on the same schedule, regardless of what the soil actually needs. This approach wastes money, damages soil health over time, and often produces worse results than a science-based approach would.

Why Soil Testing Matters

Soil is not just dirt — it is a complex living ecosystem with specific chemical, physical, and biological properties that determine how well crops grow. A soil test reveals the pH level, nutrient content, organic matter percentage, and micronutrient levels that are invisible to the naked eye but critical to plant health.

  • Over 60% of Nepal's agricultural soils are deficient in zinc and boron
  • Acidic soils (pH below 5.5) lock up nutrients even when fertilizers are applied
  • Excess nitrogen application causes groundwater contamination and crop lodging
  • Organic matter below 2% significantly reduces water retention capacity

How to Get Your Soil Tested

Soil testing in Nepal is available through district agriculture offices, private laboratories in major cities, and increasingly through mobile testing kits that provide results within 30 minutes. The cost of a comprehensive soil test ranges from NPR 500–2,000.

Krishi Connect's expert network includes soil scientists who can interpret your test results and provide specific amendment recommendations tailored to your crops and budget.

Building Soil Health Organically

The most cost-effective way to improve soil health is through organic matter additions — compost, vermicompost, green manures, and crop residue incorporation. These materials feed soil microorganisms that make nutrients available to plants, improve water retention, and build the long-term fertility that chemical fertilizers cannot provide.

A Practical Starting Point

Start with a basic pH test — inexpensive kits are available at most agricultural supply stores. If your soil pH is below 6.0, lime application will unlock nutrients that are already present but unavailable to plants. This single intervention often produces dramatic yield improvements at very low cost.
